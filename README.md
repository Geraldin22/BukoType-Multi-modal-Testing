# BukoType-Multi-modal-Testing
Classifying young coconut based on their shape, color, and tapping sound. 
In the Philippine market, selecting the right young coconut (buko) relies heavily on manual "tapping" (pukpok) and visual inspection by experienced vendors. However, this method is subjective and error-prone, leading to a high rate of mismatch between vendor predictions and actual fruit characteristics:

1. Malauhog – Thin, . gelatinous meat (ideal for UTI/health drinks)
2. Pang-salad – Thick, firm meat (ideal for salads/desserts)
3. Maraming Sabaw – High liquid volume ratio
4. Malakanin – Medium-thick meat (ideal for direct consumption/snacking)

Misclassification results in product wastage, financial losses for vendors, and customer dissatisfaction. To address this gap, this study proposes a non-invasive, objective classification system using a multimodal approach that integrates acoustic signal analysis and visual feature extraction.
